# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences arrive at the same site, and each needs its own door.

1. **Small local business owners in Brazil**, and Brazilian businesses in Portugal: air conditioning and HVAC companies, guesthouses, martial arts gyms, dance and music schools, pet care, car detailing, and similar services. They usually have only Instagram or a Google profile. Most arrive from a WhatsApp message with a link to the demo of their segment, from Google, or from an AI assistant. They are not technical, read on a phone, and decide on price, speed, trust, and whether a real person will answer them.
2. **Companies that need software**: founders, product and operations leaders, engineering managers, and agencies evaluating a senior freelance engineer for B2B systems, internal tools, modernization, mobile, and applied AI. They arrive from search, referrals, LinkedIn, GitHub, or an AI agent and need to understand fit, evidence, availability, and contact path quickly.

## Product Purpose

Rafael Sant' Anna is a senior software engineer (currently full time at Fundacred, so the site offers freelance work only) who sells two things:

- **Websites for small businesses** with a fixed scope and visible price: a page ready in 3 business days from R$ 700 (guesthouses with booking requests from R$ 1,200), domain and hosting for the first year, a WhatsApp button, and preparation for Google and AI search. The `/sites/` hub and one page per segment (`/sites/<niche>/`) carry the offer. All prices live in `src/data/small-business.ts`.
- **Systems, apps, and AI** for companies, quoted by scope.

Because client work cannot be shown, the portfolio proves capability through working demonstrations he built himself, each with a fictional brand, fictional data, and an explicit disclosure: five systems and seven local-business landing pages. Real experience (Fundacred, Affinity/Axians, Revvo, Trinks, aspecTI, EdgeData) is presented as text in About and CV.

Success means a business owner opens the model of their segment and messages on WhatsApp, and a company tries a demo, understands which service fits, and starts a conversation. The site must also expose clear, factual, machine-readable content for search engines and agents.

## Brand Personality

Inventive, personal, and precise. The voice is specific, commercially useful, and honest. With business owners it speaks plain Brazilian Portuguese (price, deadline, WhatsApp, Pix) and avoids technical terms; with companies it can be technical without posturing. The portfolio frame is restrained, dark by default with an equivalent light theme, with a single violet accent, so each demo's own art direction carries the color. Rafael's real portrait and name appear where trust matters, especially on the small-business pages.

## Anti-references

- A cheap copy of either Ruben Marcus or Christopher Gonçalves, including their green or cyan palette and exact portrait treatment.
- Warm cream and orange palettes for the portfolio frame itself. Individual demos may use any palette their brand needs.
- Generic AI tool marketing with neon gradients, gradient text, glass panels, radial spotlight glows, vague claims, and empty futuristic decoration.
- Editorial-magazine styling for the portfolio frame, with display serifs, tiny mono labels, and ornamental rules.
- Repeated identical card grids and framework-logo walls without evidence or context; technical jargon placed in the path of a business owner.
- Screenshots of work that cannot be shown, or diagrams pretending to be evidence.
- The legacy Simplefolio layout, generic developer slogans, and unclear calls to action.
- Fictional clients, testimonials, ratings, inflated metrics, implied endorsements, visible lists of search phrases, or other keyword stuffing.

## Design Principles

1. **Two doors.** Every key page lets a business owner reach the website offer and WhatsApp, and lets a company reach systems and the project brief, without either audience wading through the other's content.
2. **Try before trust.** Every demo can be opened and used. Cards show what each demo does (the phone preview scrolling to WhatsApp, the live miniature of a system), not only a picture of it.
3. **Honest by construction.** Demonstrations carry a visible authorial disclosure and fictional data. Prices are real starting prices; nothing promises a Google position. Real experience stays CV-backed.
4. **Commercial clarity.** Price, deadline, what is included, payment, and who answers are visible before contact. The Portuguese WhatsApp number is explained next to WhatsApp actions aimed at Brazil.
5. **Human and machine readable.** Semantic HTML, concise facts, structured data (Service with Offer, FAQPage), Markdown alternatives, and complete bilingual content are one system.
6. **A quiet frame for colorful work.** The frame stays restrained in both themes so each demo stands out. Motion and interaction prove craft and never hide content.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes. All content and actions must work with keyboard, screen readers, 200% zoom, reduced motion, touch, and high-contrast environments. Body text stays at least 16px, touch targets at least 44px, and meaning never depends on color or hover alone.
